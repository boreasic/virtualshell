How VirtualShell handles data.

VirtualShell provides a local Linux environment on iPhone and iPad. This page explains the app’s built-in data handling and where your own commands can send information.

Data on your device

VirtualShell keeps its Linux roots, terminal state, and app settings on your device. The app does not require a Boreasic account and does not include an advertising or analytics SDK. Files you expose through the iOS Files app are available to other apps when you choose to open or share them.

Optional location

A guest program receives coordinates only while it reads /dev/location. VirtualShell requests iOS When In Use permission when a foreground command first needs access. An authorized active reader can continue receiving updates in the background, with the iOS location indicator visible. Closing the last reader stops location updates. Denying permission makes the read fail.

Coordinates are delivered to the local guest program. A program you run may send them to a network service; review the programs and commands you use.

Network connections

Programs in the guest can connect to services you choose. Package installation contacts configured package repositories, which may receive your IP address and ordinary request information. VirtualShell also makes a request to an Apple network endpoint at launch to trigger iOS local-network permission behavior on devices where that prompt is required. Optional Codex remote control and custom server features connect or listen only when enabled by the user; external services used by those features have their own privacy practices.

Support requests

If you open a support issue, the information you choose to post is sent to GitHub and may be public. Do not include secrets, private files, or precise location data. GitHub’s privacy policy applies to that service.

Your choices

You can deny location permission in iOS Settings, stop a command reading location with Ctrl-C, remove files and roots you no longer need, and disable optional server features in the app. iOS controls app backups and deletion.

Questions

For privacy questions, open a support issue without including private data. This policy may be updated when the app’s behavior changes.

AT A GLANCE

Local by default

Files and terminal state stay on device unless you choose to move them or run a program that sends data. Location starts only for an active reader and follows the iOS permission and indicator.
